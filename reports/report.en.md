## 6. DVF

Among 100 DVF candidates, the number with a missing axis is D 0, F 0, V 90. The riskiest axis is V for 91, F for 4 and D for 5 candidates, 90 of them because the axis is missing. Related patents are scarce, so V is mostly missing, and the missing-axis-first rule (design §8.4) concentrates the MVP type on customer interviews (design §16 item 20).

**Ranking stability (Poisson bootstrap over evidence units, design §7.3):** of 210 non-missing axis scores, 115 have a bootstrap interval spanning 2 or more score levels. Of 10 candidates with all three axes, 10 have their riskiest axis picked again in less than 0.95 of replicates (lowest share 0.23), 2 of them briefing targets. Designations made because an axis is missing are fixed, but where the riskiest axis is chosen by comparing scores the MVP type is sensitive to the evidence sample (design §16 item 23).

## 7. Briefings and quality

- Generation model gemini-3.5-flash-lite. Of 100 frame generations, 56 were complete, 44 insufficient and 0 invalid.
- Reasons for insufficient concepts: no combination evidence 20, term mismatch 14, comparison only 10. Combinations whose co-occurrence reflects comparison rather than combination (design §16 item 17) and vocabulary period mismatches and acronym ambiguity (§16 item 15) were filtered at the frame stage.

**Faithfulness rests on three layers.**

1. **Structural guarantees:** per-candidate schemas restrict evidence ids to the candidate's bundle, and the DVF axis, MVP type and package versions are attached from result files rather than generated. Citing an id outside the bundle or changing a DVF value cannot happen.
2. **Preregistered measurement:**
   - Q1 faithfulness: 61 of 100 sampled evidential sentences are supported by their cited evidence (0.610, Wilson interval [0.512, 0.700]).
   - Q2 stability: frame agreement 92 / 100 (0.920, Wilson interval [0.850, 0.959]). The MVP type is fixed by rule and agrees by construction.
   - Automatic rule checks: 31 flagged items.
3. **Post-hoc description (not used for the main conclusions):** of 31 flagged items, 7 were classified as actual violations rather than limits of the check rules. This classification was defined after seeing the flags.

| Q1 field | Supported / sample |
|---|---|
| concept_frame | 32 / 43 |
| target_user | 1 / 7 |
| value_prop | 1 / 5 |
| building_blocks | 8 / 16 |
| jtbd | 4 / 8 |
| summary | 15 / 21 |


Sentences that must synthesise beyond the evidence (target user, value proposition) were supported less often than the frame sentences that summarise it. Among the sampled sentences, the automatic checks flagged 0 of the unsupported and 0 of the supported ones: rule-based checks do not detect whether the content is supported.

**Cross-model check:** Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B judged the same sample. Agreement with the human judgments 0.580, Cohen's kappa 0.156, invalid outputs 1. A small open model cannot replace the faithfulness judgment, and Q1 rests on a single reviewer (design §16 item 9).

## 8. Reinforcement learning case

Among the briefing targets, 2 are reinforcement learning candidates and 1 qualify for the deep dive (frame complete in every repetition).

- Prescription rank 2: proximal policy optimization · trajectory planning · vision language action. Frame complete 5 / 5, riskiest axis V, Q1 sample 5 / 12.
- Prescription rank 19: proximal policy optimization · efficient exploration · multi agent reinforcement learning. Frame complete 2 / 5, riskiest axis V, Q1 sample 3 / 4.


The deep-dive candidate's frame was complete in every repetition, yet its sampled sentences were supported less often than the overall sample. Repeating the same claim stably and that claim being faithful to the evidence are different questions, which is why Q2 and Q1 are measured separately. According to the review notes, most unsupported sentences widened autonomous-driving evidence to robotics or attributed the effect of one component to the whole combination.

**Contrast with tod (earlier project)** (source `external/tod/PROVENANCE.md`):

| Issue | tod | TriadRx |
|---|---|---|
| DVF discrimination | The LLM-scored DVF gave every reliable concept the same median total (1 distinct value) | The indicator-based DVF yields 25 axis-score profiles over 100 candidates |
| Leakage at the judging stage | Removing period cues moved the large judge's rank correlation with diffusion from -0.95 to 0.63 | No LLM judges promise or DVF, so that path does not exist |
| Axis without evidence | The LLM scores it anyway | Left missing and designated as the riskiest assumption |
| Judge agreement | Two models on coarse promise scores: rank correlation 0.907 | Model against human on sentence faithfulness: kappa 0.156 |

Unit (concepts against candidates), period and ground truth differ, so the numbers are not compared directly. Part of the DVF profile variety comes from missing V, and whether it relates to future outcomes is not tested. The line from tod's sample-efficient policy optimisation and robotic manipulation concepts to TriadRx's PPO-based fine-tuning of VLA policies is an interpretation, not a measurement.

## 9. Limitations

All limitations of design §16 apply. The most important for reading the results:

- Engine reliability not established (V1 not passed) and a narrow candidate range (candidate recall).
- Matching errors from M1 failing in every stratum, vocabulary gaps, vocabulary period mismatches and acronym ambiguity.
- Survivorship bias of the vocabulary (it may inflate lightweight-validation performance) and no new-term supplement (techniques that appeared after the vocabulary snapshot cannot become candidates; design §16 items 4 and 22).
- Sparse V evidence and the resulting concentration of MVP types; unstable score-based riskiest-axis designations; no assignee concentration reference; government-category classification precision not established.
- A patent data gap before the data cutoff and a shortened D download window.
- Q1 rests on one reviewer and was not corroborated by the cross-model check; some unsupported judgments come from the design (missing package descriptions, claims of absent evidence that citations cannot support).
- DVF predictive validity not tested; it will be checked through the prospective registration (design §11).

## 11. Prospective check

The prescription top 100 and a baseline of the top 100 by pair proximity (`P_aa_min`) after the same redundancy filter were registered before any outcome existed (design §11.1, preregistration section 8). The comparison population is the 218,154 candidates passing the redundancy filter, and the two lists share 0 triads. The label window starts 1 year(s) after the data cutoff and lasts 2 years; the verdict date is 2029-09-24. P1 asks whether the prescription top beats random ranking and P2 whether it beats the baseline in top precision; as in the lightweight validation, the lower bounds of both bootstraps must clear the threshold. The check script does not run before the verdict date and stops if an input file differs from its registered hash.
