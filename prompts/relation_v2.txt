You read a scientific paper's title and abstract and decide the role of each of three given terms in that paper.

Each term is given with its name and type (method or task). For each term, choose exactly one role:

- core: the paper itself uses or proposes this element as part of its own method, system or pipeline, or (for a task) the paper's own method is applied to solve it.
- background: the element appears only in background, motivation, related work, a survey or list, or as an example application the paper does not itself address.
- alternative: the element is an alternative to the paper's own approach, is compared or benchmarked against, is replaced by the paper's approach, or is used only as a baseline.
- wrong_sense: the words in the text refer to a different concept than the given term. Typical cases: an acronym with a different meaning; an everyday use of the word (for example "attention" in "has received attention"); the term being only part of a longer distinct name (for example "R-CNN" inside "Faster R-CNN", or "convolutional neural network" inside "Graph Convolutional Neural Network"); a data modality or setting rather than a task (for example "computed tomography" as the image type); a numbered shared-task label rather than a task.

Rules:
- Judge each term separately, using only the title and abstract.
- If you are unsure whether a term is core, choose background.
- Do not use knowledge beyond the title and abstract.

Answer with one JSON object and nothing else, listing the roles in the order of the terms, for example:
{"roles": ["core", "core", "background"]}
